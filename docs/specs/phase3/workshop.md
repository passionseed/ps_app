## What This Document Is

Specification for the Phase 3 ignition workshop and the 8-day clips drumbeat that follows it. Workshop is one of three teaching channels (the other two: app learning block, daily AI mentor). This doc defines its specific job and content.

**v2.0 change:** all-online format. Menti drives two-way participation. 60 min hard cap (HS attention budget). Replaces in-person pair practice with breakout-room pretotype test + Menti debrief. Adds problem-statement clinic using teams' real Phase 2 submission data.

---

## Job

Workshop is **ignition, not teaching**. 60 min cannot teach iteration discipline. Job is:

1. **Reframe what wins** — judges reward validated pain-point solving, not polished apps.
2. **Confront PS quality honestly** — most Phase 2 problem statements are weak. Make this visible, anonymously, and fixable.
3. **Identity shift** — teams stop thinking "we're building an app," start thinking "we're running experiments."
4. **Anchor 3 sticky phrases** hard enough to survive 10 days alone.
5. **Run one viscerally memorable demo** that shows iteration in action.
6. **Practice observation discipline** in a breakout, low stakes.
7. **Extract public commitment** with stakes.

Methodology lives in the app. Workshop opens the door. Menti makes the door two-way.

## What Wins — Core Frame

Open with this. Hard.

> "What wins Round 1 is **not** the prettiest app. **Not** the most AI features. **Not** the fanciest demo. What wins is: a real pain point, a pretotype that targets that pain, and **evidence from real target users that it actually moves the needle.** Judges read 'Problem Statement' and 'Solution Effectiveness' as 'how much real user evidence did you collect.' If you can't show evidence, you don't win. End of story."

## The Big Demo — Online Script (Block 0:20–0:32, 12 min)

Rehearse with planted user on Zoom 3x before live. Pre-load both pretotype versions in tabs ready to switch.

### Setup (1 min)
Instructor shares screen with pretotype. Planted user is hackathon staff member, not pre-coached on the pretotype, on Zoom video. Instructor narrates:
> "Real Phase 2 pretotype, anonymized. Hypothesis: 'students will sign up for our AI tutor if they see one free answer first.' Watch me make every mistake teams make."

### Bad test (3 min)
Instructor pitches while testing. Talks over user. Leading questions: "do you like this?" "would you use it?"
User responds vaguely positively from social pressure: "yeah it's nice."
Instructor: "Great, hypothesis confirmed!"

### Menti call-out (1 min)
Menti open-text: "What just went wrong?" Audience floods. Top hits appear on screen: leading questions, pitching, no observation, social pressure. Instructor: "Right. Garbage. Watch the rewrite."

### Good test (3 min)
Same pretotype, same user. Instructor:
> "Show me how you'd find an answer to a homework question right now. Don't explain, just do it."

User opens. Hesitates at signup. Tries to skip. Can't. Frowns. Closes tab.
Instructor (silent throughout): "Why'd you close it?"
User: "I don't sign up before I know what it is."

### Variable isolation via Menti (2 min)
Menti ranking: "Which ONE variable to change first?" Options: remove signup gate / show one free answer / change copy / redesign flow / add demo video. Audience ranks. Top vote wins. Instructor: "Five guesses. Pick ONE. Test it. If it doesn't move the needle, you've eliminated a hypothesis. Science."

### Re-test (2 min)
Switch to modified pretotype tab. Same user.
> "Show me how you'd find an answer right now."

User opens. Sees free answer. Reads. Smiles. Asks "is there more?" Sees signup. Signs up.

### Lesson (sticky moment)
Instructor: "Same pretotype. Same user. ONE variable changed. Different result. **Pretotype is the question. User is the answer. One variable per cycle. Shut up and watch.** Three things. That's the workshop."

## Problem Statement Clinic — Details (Block 0:08–0:20)

### Source data
Pre-pull 8–10 problem statements from Phase 2 submissions before workshop. Anonymize (no team name, no specifics that identify). Pick a mix: 2 strong, 4 weak, 2 fixable mid. Have them slide-ready.

### Anatomy of a weak PS (teach this fast, 2 min)
A strong PS has:
- **WHO** specific user (not "students" — "Grade 11 students in Bangkok prepping for SAT")
- **WHAT pain** observable behavior or outcome (not "they're stressed" — "they spend 3+ hours hunting practice problems across 5 sites")
- **WHY current solutions fail** (not "no good tools" — "Khan Academy too broad, paid tutors $40/hr, parents can't help with SAT-specific")
- **WHAT changes if solved** measurable (not "less stressed" — "cut practice-problem hunting time by 50%")

If PS doesn't have all 4, it's weak.

### Anchor examples — pulled from real Phase 2 submissions (anonymized)

These three examples drive the clinic. Slide each one. Vote on Menti. Reveal.

**Example A — WEAK (use first, sets the floor)**
> "เด็กนักเรียนในโรงเรียนกำลังมีภาวะ ADHD เพราะผู้ปกครองปล่อยปละละเลย ส่งผลให้เด็กนักเรียนเป็นโรค ADHD"

Why weak (instructor breakdown, 90 sec):
- **WHO**: "เด็กนักเรียนในโรงเรียน" — which school, which grade, how many, where? Unknown.
- **WHAT pain**: collapses cause and effect ("เป็น ADHD ส่งผลให้เป็น ADHD") — circular.
- **Causal claim wrong**: ADHD is neurological, not caused by parental neglect. PS makes medical claim it can't back. Judges will spot this.
- **No measurement**: nothing to validate against.
- **Verdict**: weak in every dimension. Also factually risky.

**Example B — STRONG REFLECTION (use second, shows the bar)**
> "ตอนแรกที่เชื่อว่าเด็กไม่อ่านหนังสือเพราะ 'ขี้เกียจไม่อดทน' ตอนนี้รู้ความจริงแล้วว่าระบบทำให้ 'สมองพวกเขารับภาระหนักเกินไป (Cognitive Overload)' จนร่างกายชัตดาวน์ไปเอง"

Why strong (instructor breakdown, 90 sec):
- Names the **mechanism** (cognitive overload) not just symptom (not reading).
- Shows **mental model update** — old belief vs new evidence-shaped belief.
- Specific cause → specific intervention possible.
- Still missing WHO + WHAT measurement — not a full PS yet, but the diagnostic clarity is the model.

**Example C — STRONG SPECIFIC (use third, shows the gold)**
> "พนักงานออฟฟิศที่นั่งทำงานนาน เกิดออฟฟิศซินโดรมจากการนั่งผิดท่าและกล้ามเนื้อบาดเจ็บสะสม — ระบบเตือนพักเดิม (timer) ไม่ตอบโจทย์ เพราะไม่ตรวจจับท่าทาง ไม่นำเสนอวิธียืดเหยียดที่ถูกต้อง"

Why strong (instructor breakdown, 90 sec):
- **WHO** specific (office workers, prolonged sitting).
- **WHAT pain** specific behavior + mechanism (wrong posture, cumulative muscle injury).
- **WHY current fails** specific (timer-based, no posture detection, no correct stretch).
- **WHAT changes** implied (posture-triggered correct intervention).
- Three of four dimensions. Strong.

### Flow
1. Show Example A. Menti MC: weak / medium / strong. Reveal vote. Instructor breakdown.
2. Show Example B. Vote. Breakdown.
3. Show Example C. Vote. Breakdown.
4. Menti open-text: "Paste your Cycle 1 PS here, anonymous." Live collect.
5. Instructor picks 2 weakest from live, rewrites live on screen 90 sec each.

Time discipline: 12 min hard cap. Cut last live-rewrite if running over.

## Breakout Room Setup (Block 0:34–0:50)

### Pre-workshop
- Collect each team's Phase 2 pretotype URL + current Cycle 1 hypothesis via form by May 10 23:59
- Zoom breakout pre-assigned: pair teams of opposite tracks where possible (HS↔Uni cross-pollination), 2 teams per room (~4–8 people total)
- Each room has Menti URL pinned + pretotype URLs pinned in chat

### In-room script (auto-displayed when room opens)

```
BREAKOUT — Peer Pretotype Review (16 min)

You are NOT your partner's target user.
Your job: poke holes in their hypothesis. Find the weakest link.
Their job: listen. No defending. No pitching.

Round 1 (7 min) — Team A presents:
  (1 min) Pretotype: what is it, who is it for
  (1 min) Cycle 1 hypothesis: WHO will DO X BECAUSE Y, MEASURED BY Z
  (1 min) Evidence they have so far + what's missing
  (4 min) Team B challenge:
    - Pick ONE hole-poking question (where would this fall apart?)
    - Suggest ONE variable to isolate first
    - Team A does NOT defend. Writes it down. Says thank you.

Round 2 (7 min) — Swap. Team B presents, Team A challenges.

Post to Menti (2 min) — EACH team posts:
  - Team name
  - Cycle 1 hypothesis (revised if needed after challenge)
  - ONE variable changing next cycle
  - ONE evidence target by May 15 (e.g., "5 target users tested, 3 returned next day")

Return to main room at timer.
```

### Host moderator role
2 facilitators float between rooms via Zoom co-host. Drop in, listen 30 sec, leave. Flag any room where someone is pitching or defending instead of listening.

## Said / Did / Returned — Signal Hierarchy for HS

Workshop uses **Said / Did / Returned** instead of classic Said / Did / Paid. HS users don't pay money. "Returned" = highest-cost behavioral signal available.

| Signal | What it looks like | Evidence weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Said** | "Cool, I'd use it" / "looks nice" / "would help me" | Weakest. Social pressure. Discount heavily. |
| **Did** | Completed task in pretotype without help. Clicked, navigated, finished flow without prompting. | Medium. Behavior > opinion. |
| **Returned** | Came back next day unprompted. Gave real contact (LINE / IG / email). Brought a friend. Asked when next version drops. Stayed longer than asked. Shared organically. Signed up with real email/phone. | Strongest. Costly signal. **This is "paid" for HS hackathon.** |

Teams that report only Said-evidence in submission video are flagged for low-quality evidence (judge brief). Teams that capture Did + Returned signals win.

### How to capture Returned evidence
- Send follow-up message 24h after first test. Log who replies vs ignores.
- Ask at end of test: "want me to send you the next version when it's ready?" Log who gives real contact.
- Track unprompted return visits to pretotype URL (link analytics).
- Count organic shares (screenshot if shared without ask).
- Recruit 1 → ask "would your friend want to try?" → log if friend actually shows up.

## Top 5 Phase 2 Mistakes (Pulled from Real Submissions)

Optional 2-min teaching block. Surface only if time. Otherwise drop into clips drumbeat.

1. **Hypothesis-test mismatch** — Hypothesis says "test 6hr no-social intervention with 20 students." Test report shows IG consult account with 5 followers. Two unrelated experiments stapled together. **Fix**: ONE hypothesis = ONE test = ONE measurable outcome.
2. **Wrong causal claim in PS** — "Children have ADHD because parents neglect them." ADHD is neurological, not parental. **Fix**: don't make medical/causal claims you can't back. Stick to observable behavior.
3. **"Wizard of Oz" labeled but not done** — Team wrote method = Wizard of Oz, but actually just made an Instagram account and waited. WoZ requires a human manually simulating the product behind a fake interface. **Fix**: read the method definition before claiming it.
4. **n=5 with no target-user filter** — Tested with whoever DMed. No filter for "is this person actually in our target group?" **Fix**: define target user in PS. Refuse non-target evidence in submission.
5. **All Said, no Did, no Returned** — Submission lists "3/5 followed account, 2/5 DMed for consultation." Following an account is barely a Did. Asking once is not Returned. **Fix**: design follow-up mechanism into Cycle 1. Capture return behavior.
