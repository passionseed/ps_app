# Market Research Report: High School Internship & Portfolio Building in Thailand

**Date:** May 1, 2026
**Prepared by:** PassionSeed Research

---

## Executive Summary

Thailand's university admission system (TCAS) creates massive demand for "portfolio-worthy" activities among ~400,000+ ม.6 students annually. Round 1 (Portfolio) is the most desirable admission path — no exam scores needed, earliest timeline, and top universities all participate. Yet the ecosystem serving these students is deeply fragmented, inequitable, and ripe for disruption.

This report maps the current landscape, identifies structural gaps, and highlights white space opportunities — particularly in light of the Thai government's upcoming TCASFolio and TCAS Verified systems launching for TCAS70 (2027 intake).

---

## 1. Market Context

### 1.1 The TCAS System Drives Everything

Thailand's university admission runs through TCAS (Thai University Central Admission System), managed by CUPT (Council of University Presidents). It consists of 4 rounds:

| Round | Name | Method | Timeline |
|-------|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Portfolio** | Achievements, activities, certificates — no exam scores | Aug–Nov |
| 2 | Quota | Regional/talent-based selection | Dec–Mar |
| 3 | Admission | National exam scores (TGAT, TPAT, A-Level) | May |
| 4 | Direct Admission | Leftover seats filled by universities | Jun |

**Round 1 Portfolio is the most desirable path** — it's the earliest, doesn't require exam scores, and top faculties at Chulalongkorn, Thammasat, Mahidol, KMITL, Kasetsart, and others all participate. This creates massive demand for "portfolio-worthy" activities.

## 2. How Students Do It Now (Current Behavior)

### 2.1 The Student Journey

**ม.4 (Grade 10) — Low Awareness**
- Most students have zero awareness of portfolio needs
- Some elite Bangkok schools start guiding students early
- Rural/provincial schools provide almost no guidance

**ม.5 (Grade 11) — Panic & Scramble**
- Students realize they need activities for portfolio
- Typical discovery methods:
  - Ask friends and seniors what they did
  - Browse Dek-D.com forums
  - Search Facebook groups for volunteer/activity announcements
  - Follow TikTok/Twitter accounts that share TCAS tips
  - Ask school teachers (quality varies wildly by school)

**ม.6 (Grade 12) — Portfolio Assembly**
- Use Canva to design portfolio PDF (most common tool)
- Hire freelancers on Facebook/Twitter to design it (฿500–5,000+)
- Use paid portfolio services — companies that both design AND help fabricate/embellish activities (฿3,000–30,000+)
- Copy templates from Dek-D or YouTube tutorials
- Submit through each university's individual system (soon to be standardized via TCASFolio)

### 2.2 Current Platforms & Tools

| Platform | What It Does | What It Doesn't Do |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Dek-D TCAS** (app + web) | TCAS news, score calculator, faculty info, countdown timers, ranking helper | No activity discovery, no portfolio building, no internship matching |
| **TCASter** (by Skooldio) | Score calculator, exam prep, faculty matching | No activity/experience side |
| **CoachDD** (by Dek-D) | Online 1-on-1 consulting with advisors | Expensive, not scalable, focused on exam strategy not activity building |
| **Canva** | Portfolio design templates | Generic tool, no TCAS-specific structure, no verification |
| **Facebook Groups** | Activity/volunteer opportunity sharing | Fragmented, unverified, hard to search, ephemeral posts |
| **YouTube / TikTok** | Portfolio tips, "how I got into X faculty" stories | Entertainment-focused, no actionable matching |
| **Paid portfolio services** (freelancers) | Design + sometimes content fabrication | Expensive, ethically questionable, government cracking down |

### 2.3 The Consulting & Tutoring Layer

| Segment | Players | Price Range | Target |
|---------|---------|-------------|--------|
| Free / low-cost | Dek-D forums, YouTube, school teachers | Free | Mass market |
| Mid-tier Thai | CoachDD (Dek-D) | ฿1,000+/session | Middle class |
| Premium Thai | The Planner Education, A. League Consulting | ฿10,000–100,000+ | Upper-middle |
| International | InGenius Prep, Crimson Education | ฿100,000–1,000,000+/year | Wealthy / international school |

**Key insight:** There is a massive gap between "free but low quality" and "high quality but expensive." The average Thai family earning ฿20,000–50,000/month has no viable option.

## 3. Available Internship Programs (Accessibility Analysis)

### 3.1 Programs That Exist

| Program | Field | Duration | Cost | Language | Location | Accessible? |
|---------|-------|----------|------|----------|----------|-------------|
| **CMKL University** | AI, robotics, IoT, cybersecurity | 8 weeks, 40 hrs/week | Free | English | Bangkok | ❌ Full-time only, English, Bangkok |
| **Absolute Internship** | Various (marketing, finance, IT, etc.) | 4–8 weeks | Paid (USD thousands) | English | Remote or Bangkok | ❌ Expensive, English |
| **GLA Thailand** | Medicine, health | 2–3 weeks | USD 4,000–6,000+ | English | Chiang Mai | ❌ Very expensive, English |
| **Rustic Pathways** | Conservation, service | 1–3 weeks | USD 2,000–5,000+ | English | Various | ❌ Expensive, English |
| **AIESEC Global Volunteer** | Education, SDGs | 6 weeks | Moderate | English | Various | ⚠️ Designed for university students |
| **Friends for Asia** | Medical shadowing, engineering | 1–4 weeks | Paid | English | Chiang Mai | ❌ Expensive, English |

### 3.2 Why These Are Not Accessible to Most Thai Students

1. **Language barrier:** Almost all structured programs are English-medium, targeting international students
2. **Cost:** ฿50,000–200,000+ for most programs — unaffordable for average families
3. **Duration:** 8+ weeks full-time is impossible during the school year
4. **Location:** Concentrated in Bangkok and Chiang Mai
5. **Age requirements:** Many require 16+ or are designed for university students
6. **Awareness:** Most Thai students don't know these programs exist
7. **Application complexity:** Require resumes, statements of purpose, transcripts — skills most Thai high schoolers don't have

**Bottom line:** There is effectively no accessible, Thai-language, affordable internship pipeline for the average ม.ปลาย student.

## 4. Gaps & White Space

### Gap 1: No Central Activity/Internship Discovery Platform

**Problem:** There is no Thai-language platform that aggregates internship, volunteer, competition, and research opportunities specifically for high school students and maps them to TCAS Round 1 requirements.

**Current workaround:** Word-of-mouth, scattered Facebook posts, school connections, Dek-D forums.

**White space:** A Thai-language activity marketplace that matches students to verified opportunities based on:
- Target faculty
- Location / province
- Available time (weekends, holidays, after school)
- Budget (free → paid)
- Duration (1 day → 8 weeks)

**Comparable:** No direct Thai competitor. Closest international analogues: Handshake (US, college-level), InnerView (US, volunteer tracking).

---

### Gap 2: The Bangkok vs. Provincial Divide

**Problem:** Portfolio Round 1 structurally favors wealthy Bangkok students who have access to international competitions, university labs, corporate internships, well-connected teachers, and portfolio design services. Provincial students have access to almost none of this.

**Data:**
- Rural students score 53–70 points lower on average
- Small rural schools lack adequate teachers and infrastructure (Springer research)
- UNICEF warns of "a permanent underclass of undereducated youth"

**White space:** Remote/online activities, micro-internships, virtual mentorship, and project-based experiences that don't require being in Bangkok. A platform that surfaces opportunities available in every province.

---

### Gap 3: No Portfolio Content Strategy Tool

**Problem:** Students know they need a portfolio but don't know **what to put in it** for their specific target faculty. Canva solves the design problem, not the content problem.

**Current workaround:** Copy what seniors did, ask on Dek-D forums, hire expensive consultants.

**White space:** A guided portfolio builder that:
- Takes the student's target faculty as input
- Shows what past successful applicants did (anonymized data)
- Recommends specific activities, competitions, and projects to pursue
- Tracks progress over ม.4–ม.6 (2–3 year journey)
- Outputs a TCASFolio-compatible submission

---

### Gap 4: Verification & Trust Infrastructure

**Problem:** The government is actively cracking down on fake portfolios and paid fabrication services. Bangkok Post has reported cases of students paying agencies to falsify profiles.

**Government response (TCAS70):**
- **TCASFolio:** Standardized central platform for portfolio submission
- **TCAS Verified:** System for schools/organizations to certify student activities directly

**But:** The infrastructure doesn't exist yet — most organizations (NGOs, companies, local groups) have no way to digitally verify student participation.

**White space:** A verification layer that connects activity providers with a digital certification system. First-mover advantage before TCASFolio becomes mandatory. Become the "activity verification backbone" that feeds into TCAS Verified.

---

### Gap 5: Affordable Guidance for the Mass Market

**Problem:** The consulting market is bifurcated with nothing in the middle:

| Tier | Quality | Price | Audience |
|------|---------|-------|----------|
| Free | Low (forums, YouTube) | ฿0 | Everyone |
| **GAP** | **—** | **฿99–499/month** | **Average Thai family** |
| Premium | High (1-on-1 consulting) | ฿1,000–10,000+/session | Upper-middle class |
| Elite | Very high (international firms) | ฿100,000+/year | Wealthy |

**White space:** AI-powered or community-driven guidance at ฿99–499/month that gives personalized, faculty-specific advice. Think "Duolingo for portfolio building" — structured, gamified, affordable, mobile-first.

---

### Gap 6: Short-Duration, Flexible Experiences

**Problem:** Most legitimate programs require 8+ weeks full-time, physical presence in Bangkok, and age 16+. Thai high school students have school 5 days/week, get ~2 months summer break (March–May), and many work part-time to support their family.

**White space:** Micro-internships (1–2 weeks), weekend projects, online research assistantships, and project-based experiences that fit around a Thai school schedule. No existing platform offers this format.

## 5. Regulatory Tailwinds

The Thai government is actively reshaping this space, creating both opportunity and urgency:

| Policy / System | Status | Impact |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| **TCASFolio** | Launching TCAS70 (July 2026) | Standardized portfolio submission — kills the "pretty PDF design" market, shifts value to actual content/activities |
| **TCAS Verified** | Launching TCAS70 | Digital activity verification — creates demand for platforms that can issue verifiable credentials |
| **Portfolio round capped at 30%** | Requested by CUPT | Universities asked to limit Round 1 seats, making each portfolio more competitive |
| **25% fee reduction for EEF students** | Active | Government acknowledging the cost barrier for disadvantaged students |
| **Crackdown on commercial portfolio services** | Active | Government explicitly wants to "discourage commercial portfolio services" — paid fabrication market will shrink |
| **Exam schedule moved to January** | TCAS70 onwards | More time between portfolio round and exams, potentially increasing portfolio round importance |

**Key implication:** The government is simultaneously:
1. Making the portfolio round more standardized and verified (TCASFolio + TCAS Verified)
2. Trying to make it more equitable (fee reductions, crackdown on paid services)
3. But NOT solving the fundamental problem of how students discover and access activities

This creates a clear opening for a platform that helps students build legitimate, verified portfolios affordably.

## 7. Opportunity Thesis

The biggest white space is a **Thai-language platform that covers the entire portfolio journey:**

```
1. DISCOVER  →  What activities/experiences does my target faculty value?
2. FIND      →  What opportunities are available to me (location, time, budget)?
3. DO        →  Participate, track progress, get verified credentials
4. BUILD     →  Assemble into a TCASFolio-compatible portfolio
5. SUBMIT    →  Guided submission with faculty-specific optimization
```

**Why now:**
- TCASFolio launching July 2026 creates a format standard to build against
- TCAS Verified creates demand for digital activity verification
- Government crackdown on fake portfolios creates demand for legitimate alternatives
- No incumbent owns this space — Dek-D is focused on content/media, not tools
- Mobile penetration in Thailand is 90%+ — the audience is reachable
- The pain is acute, recurring annually, and affects hundreds of thousands of families

**Why it's defensible:**
- Network effects: more activity providers → more students → more providers
- Data moat: understanding which activities lead to admission at which faculties
- Verification infrastructure: becoming the trusted credential layer
- Government alignment: solving the exact problem CUPT is trying to address

---

## 8. Key Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| TCASFolio could become a full platform itself | Government platforms historically focus on submission, not discovery/guidance — build the complementary layer |
| Dek-D could expand into this space | Dek-D is a media company; building a two-sided marketplace + verification system is a different competency |
| Universities could reject standardization | CUPT is driving this top-down; individual university resistance is unlikely to block the trend |
| Students may not pay for guidance | Freemium model — free activity discovery, paid personalized guidance and portfolio builder |
| Activity providers may not participate | Start with existing organizations (NGOs, university labs, government programs) that already want student participants |
